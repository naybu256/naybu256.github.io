## The takeaway

Phone Farming describes infrastructure for operating accounts across mobile environments. AGD describes a distribution model: the two categories are not synonyms.

## The argument

An equipment inventory explains available capacity, not what accounts say. Start with audiences, access rights and editorial responsibilities. Determine execution needs afterward. Buying capacity before knowing who publishes what often turns creative uncertainty into a fixed cost.

A team can have little equipment and clearly separated editorial roles. Another can possess substantial capacity without knowing what to publish. Compare systems on delivery times, reliability and the quality of delivered creative work. Hardware then answers a measured constraint rather than becoming scenery for a growth presentation. A photograph of devices cannot reveal whether the underlying accounts have a reason to exist.

## Infrastructure is a capacity, not an editorial thesis.

A room of devices can make a complicated operation visible. That visibility is seductive: people can count phones, assign accounts and imagine a larger version. The harder questions remain off camera. Which audience does each account serve? What is different about its creative work? Who checks rights, access and publication status? If those questions have no answer, adding hardware increases the number of places where an unclear decision can be executed.

Map the work before choosing the equipment. Separate research, creative production, account operation, review and measurement. Some bottlenecks may be technical, while others are simply missing ownership or a weak brief. Estimate the cost of supervising a larger network, including failed jobs and corrections. This does not make infrastructure unimportant. Reliable execution matters enormously once the team knows what it is trying to execute. It means the purchase decision should follow a defined operating need, rather than becoming the evidence that a distribution strategy already exists.

### What sits around the publishing action

- **Editorial:** Audience promise, concept and evidence.

- **Operations:** Access, asset checks, execution and incident ownership.

- **Learning:** A consistent record of what happened and why it matters.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
