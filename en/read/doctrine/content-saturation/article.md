## The takeaway

Content Saturation describes content supply exceeding available attention. Within AGD it is a thesis about competition, not an established universal measurement.

## The argument

Your actual competitor in a feed might be a joke a friend shared. A post does not win merely by beating another company’s post. It must earn a place in a session where nobody requested your product. Situational relevance becomes more useful than another arbitrary posting schedule.

For a tutorial, narrowing the promise may be the useful response. “Find a note during a call” describes a real moment; “be more productive” describes almost everything. A specific situation helps choose the opening screen, what to demonstrate and what to remove. Competition becomes a relevance question rather than a demand to make every piece louder, faster and longer.

## Compete with the viewer’s next choice, not your rival’s calendar.

Your next post does not enter an empty lane reserved for products in your category. It enters a session containing a message from a friend, a joke, a useful tutorial and a dozen things the viewer never asked to see. That changes the brief. “Our feature is better” may be true and still be irrelevant to the moment. Describe the situation in which the viewer would care, then make that situation recognisable before asking them to process the product.

Audit a proposed post by removing its introduction. If the useful part begins several sentences later, the viewer is paying an attention tax to reach it. Sometimes context is necessary, especially for a difficult explanation; the question is whether the context creates understanding or merely delays the point. Compare a concise version with a version that builds useful tension, using similar audiences and a consistent observation window. Do not turn content saturation into a universal explanation for weak results. It is a way to sharpen editorial choices, not a diagnosis you can attach to every declining graph.

### Earn the next few seconds

- **Recognise:** Make a specific situation legible immediately.

- **Deliver:** Give the viewer information or a feeling worth staying for.

- **Continue:** Make the next action a natural extension of that value.
