Interpretation: A transformation only rewards recognition when the original is clear. If the viewer cannot identify the source, the reveal loses the comparison that makes it satisfying.

## The argument

A model may group videos by background while their promises differ. Define angle, format and situation. Review a sample of human-machine disagreements. The goal is not a perfect taxonomy but recoverable, useful creative decisions.

Use a few annotated examples as references and retain cases belonging to several groups. An ambiguous category is better than artificial precision. If the agent mostly classifies scenery, rewrite the instruction around the problem addressed. Grouping should help retrieve ideas rather than merely fill a metadata column. A small review set can reveal whether the labels preserve useful distinctions across genuinely different creative treatments.

## Let the machine organise; keep the labels interpretable

Clustering can help a team find repeated ideas in a large creative library, but a cluster is not automatically a meaningful strategy category. Similar colours or wording may pull together posts that solve different audience problems. Inspect representative items and name the group in terms an editor can use: objection handled, proof shown, narrative structure or product use case. Keep the original metadata and allow manual corrections. If nobody can explain why two posts belong together, the cluster is difficult to turn into a useful brief.

Use the groups to find gaps and concentration, not to declare causal winners. A high-performing cluster may contain older posts, stronger accounts or a different observation window. Compare those factors before attributing outcomes to the creative pattern. Preserve a place for outliers rather than forcing every unusual idea into the nearest familiar bucket. The library should make it easier to discover a fresh question, not only to reproduce the most common visual style. Review the labels as the editorial programme changes; yesterday's useful taxonomy can become today's blind spot.

The LEGO Reel can sit in more than one sensible editorial group: product reinterpretation, fashion collaboration or visual analogy. That is a reminder that a cluster depends on the question being asked. If the library is meant to inspire product demonstrations, label the transformation of source material. If it is meant to study collaborations, label what the second creative world contributes. Keep both tags when useful rather than forcing one universal taxonomy. A machine can propose these relationships, but an editor should be able to explain why a future brief would benefit from seeing the reference.

### From similarity to an editorial decision

- **Group:** Find candidate patterns without treating them as truth.

- **Label:** Name the audience problem or creative mechanism.

- **Inspect:** Review exceptions and compare like conditions.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
