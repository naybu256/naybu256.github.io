THE SIGNAL

Diversity also lives in what you learn.

A creative-diversity test examines meaningful variation rather than counting files.

1 / 3 · YOUR OWN PACE

Label angles, personas and situations before examining performance. Compare similar-volume groups with documented diversity differences. Keep costs visible: more angles may require more design work. Ask whether the difference improves useful learning, not merely outcome dispersion.

A blind review can help: hide results and ask several people to classify creative work using the taxonomy. Disagreements expose vague categories. This does not measure marketing impact, but checks whether the definition of diversity can be applied without already knowing winners. Keep ambiguous cases visible and revise the coding guide before using those categories to explain performance or allocate the next production budget.

Count distinct hypotheses, not exported files

A folder of thirty edits can contain one idea. Different subtitles, crops and backing tracks may help optimise execution, but they do not necessarily test different reasons for someone to care. Label each creative by the audience problem, claim, proof and narrative device before counting diversity. A price objection, a workflow demonstration and a surprising use case are meaningfully different propositions even when they use the same visual template. This distinction prevents a large production queue from masquerading as a broad exploration of demand.

Choose a manageable set of hypotheses and give each enough execution quality to be a fair attempt. Keep the downstream destination coherent so a successful opening does not lead to an unrelated offer. After publication, review the comments for evidence about interpretation as well as the performance metrics. A low-view post that reveals a serious misunderstanding may improve the next brief. Preserve the labels and failed attempts in the creative library. Otherwise the team repeatedly rediscovers the same idea under new filenames and calls the repetition a learning system.

The sink-cleaning example makes sound part of the stated creative treatment. A useful exploration would compare genuinely different ways of explaining a relevant task, not merely swap the music while keeping every promise identical. One treatment might emphasise the transformation, another the difficult step and another the sensory process. Those are candidate hypotheses about what makes the activity interesting. Give each an original, rights-cleared execution and define the intended outcome before publishing. The presence of a named product in the reference is observable context; sponsorship and commercial effectiveness remain separate questions that the available evidence does not resolve.
