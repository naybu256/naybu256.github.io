## The takeaway

A frequency test must separate total volume, per-post performance and production load. Higher totals can coexist with weaker individual posts.

## The argument

Define two realistic cadences with comparable creative quality. Observe coordination costs and undelivered content. Do not conclude from average views alone: the network may seek more qualified visits at a manageable cost. No test result is supplied here.

A favorable total may also increase team fatigue or correction work. Record those effects in the review instead of treating them as outside the experiment. A suitable cadence depends on the whole system. Sustainable output with readable briefs may be preferable to a production spike that cannot survive the following week. This is an operating tradeoff to evaluate, not an assumption that lower frequency always performs better.

## Frequency changes the production system too

Increasing output changes more than the number of chances to reach someone. It can shorten research, reduce review, repeat ideas and make the account's promise harder to recognise. A frequency test that ignores those changes may attribute a creative quality problem to audience fatigue, or mistake extra total reach for a better process. Decide whether the question concerns reach per post, total useful reach, buyer response, or production efficiency. These outcomes can move in opposite directions without any contradiction. The team needs to know which trade-off it is willing to accept.

Keep a record of production time and distinct concepts as well as published units. Three edits of one idea are not three independent discoveries about the audience. If a higher cadence works only while a person absorbs unpaid overtime, the apparent efficiency is incomplete. Use a bounded trial with a review date, compare equally mature posts, and watch whether comments show confusion or repetition. A sustainable schedule leaves enough capacity to learn from responses. Publishing faster than the team can understand its own output is an expensive form of scrolling.

### What a cadence change really touches

- **Capacity:** Research, editing, review and response time.

- **Output:** Posts, distinct concepts and useful audience contact.

- **Cost:** Hours and spend required to sustain the routine.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
