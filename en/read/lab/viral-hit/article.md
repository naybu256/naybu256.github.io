## The takeaway

A Viral Hit is an unusually high distribution outcome relative to a defined baseline: account, cohort or campaign. A fixed view threshold does not fit every context.

## The argument

A 40,000-view video means different things for an account usually reaching 600 and one usually reaching 500,000. These numbers are illustrative. Define the baseline before observing results. Otherwise you can move the threshold until it supports the story you wanted.

Keep the selected threshold and its rationale beside the result. A history-relative threshold can become unstable when an account has published very little. A comparable cohort may then be more informative, provided its composition is justified. The word viral should remain attached to a readable baseline. If no credible reference exists yet, describe the observed views and uncertainty rather than forcing a classification.

## The hit is a lead, not a blueprint

The first useful thing to do after a breakout is surprisingly boring: freeze the evidence. Save the creative version, opening frame, caption, publication time and observation window. Record which audience the post was meant to reach before reading the comments. Otherwise the story changes around the result. A joke that happened to travel becomes a carefully engineered positioning strategy in the retrospective. The next brief then inherits an explanation nobody actually tested. Treat the successful post as a promising clue with several competing explanations: subject, execution, account history, external timing and chance.

Build the follow-up around a question small enough to answer. Keep the audience problem and change the opening, or keep the opening structure and change the example. Do not change the persona, format and offer simultaneously and call that a replication. Give the new posts comparable observation windows, include the ordinary results, and inspect whether the response came from people who could use the product. A useful hit can teach you a repeatable creative move. It cannot, by itself, tell you how frequently that move will work.

### What to do after the spike

- **Freeze:** Save the exact version and a dated observation window.

- **Explain:** List competing reasons before picking a favourite.

- **Repeat:** Test one transferable mechanism with new material.
