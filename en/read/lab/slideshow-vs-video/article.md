## The takeaway

Comparing slideshow and video requires a comparable promise and audience. Reading behavior and available metrics may differ between formats.

## The argument

Turn one question into two suitable treatments without forcing video into an animated slideshow. Choose a useful shared metric, such as measurable qualified visits. Keep format-native metrics separate. The test compares content experiences, not file extensions.

Include production cost in the table. A video may achieve a stronger raw outcome with much more work, while a slideshow may make an explanation easier to revise. The choice depends on the editorial question and resources. Present the tradeoff rather than crowning a format for every situation. Also retain whether both treatments actually delivered equivalent information; unequal substance can otherwise masquerade as a format effect.

## Compare the job before comparing the format

A slideshow and a video can serve different reader needs. A sequence of screenshots may let someone inspect a process at their own pace; a moving demonstration may make a physical transformation immediately legible. Calling one format better because its view count is higher ignores that difference in purpose. Start with a single audience question and produce two treatments that both answer it honestly. Keep the promise, offer and destination aligned. Otherwise the experiment compares different ideas wearing different file extensions.

Measure behaviour that reflects the intended job. A tutorial may deserve attention to saves, useful questions and visits to the full instructions; a product demonstration may deserve attention to qualified visits and purchases. Platform definitions and available metrics can differ, so document what each number means. Include the time required to produce a publishable version and avoid treating a low-effort first draft as representative of a format. The useful conclusion is often conditional: this treatment makes this explanation easier to understand for this audience, under the conditions observed.

### One question, two treatments

- **Slideshow:** Let the reader inspect and advance through steps.

- **Video:** Use movement, timing and sound when they add meaning.

- **Decision:** Compare comprehension and the intended downstream action.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
