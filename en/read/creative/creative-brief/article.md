## The argument

Replace “make a viral video” with “test whether this friction resonates with students revising.” List requirements, freedoms and boundaries. A useful constraint explains its role: the first screen should make the situation visible, not merely contain a keyword.

Add the sentence a viewer should be able to repeat afterward. It is not a mandatory slogan but a comprehension check. If the creative team proposes a better way to convey the idea, the brief should allow it. The document sets the job’s objective without confusing that objective with the first imagined solution. This distinction keeps constraints useful while preserving the creator’s judgment.

## A brief should make the review easier before production begins.

Write the audience situation, the claim and the evidence before choosing an effect or a format. A useful brief might ask whether a visible before-and-after helps a certain viewer understand a workflow. A weak brief asks for three viral videos with different hooks. The first gives the team a question it can answer. The second turns every result into an argument about taste, energy or whether the creator tried hard enough.

Include what must stay fixed and what the production is allowed to change. Specify the source material, rights context and the person who can approve the final version. Give examples of the desired mechanism without asking a creator to imitate someone’s identity or reproduce their clip. During review, compare the finished asset with the original learning question. A technically polished video can still fail the brief; a rough prototype can be useful if it resolves a decision before expensive production. Keep that distinction visible in how the team labels and evaluates work.

Emily Mariko's recipe is useful as a reference for a concrete transformation. The reported ingredients and the finished bowl give the viewer something specific to understand and potentially try. Translate that clarity into the brief rather than copying the setting or aesthetic. Name the starting state, the visible action and the result. Add the one detail that makes this example worth showing instead of describing in a sentence. For a software tutorial, that might be the awkward source file or the inspection step that proves the output is usable. The brief should tell an editor where the explanation becomes visible, not merely request an engaging video.

### A brief an editor can review

- **Question:** What do we want to learn about this audience or explanation?

- **Evidence:** What must the viewer actually see?

- **Boundary:** What can change, and what would invalidate the comparison?
