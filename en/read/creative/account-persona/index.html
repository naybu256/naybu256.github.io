Write the opinions this account can defend and the topics it leaves to others. A rushed student does not discuss an app like an organizational consultant. Character appears through chosen details, not a biography full of adjectives. A fictional persona should be identifiable as such when it could be mistaken for a real person.

Test the character against a frustration. What happens when the app does not solve everything? A credible voice can acknowledge a limit, propose a manual workaround or reject an unsuitable use. If the persona only praises the product, its identity may collapse when the audience asks an unexpected question. A consistent boundary often reveals more character than another carefully chosen visual accessory.

A persona is a set of editorial decisions under pressure.

Describe how the account behaves when the obvious joke does not fit. What would it notice in a room? What would it explain patiently? What would it refuse to pretend to know? Those questions produce a more usable persona than an age, a stock photograph and three adjectives. A student narrator worried about exams and a researcher worried about citations may both discuss notes, but they organise a demonstration around different stakes.

Write a short example in the persona’s voice, then write the same scene from another account’s perspective. Change the situation and the evidence, not only the slang. Ask an editor to identify each intended viewer without seeing the labels. If they cannot, the brief probably needs more specificity. A fictional or synthetic character can still have a consistent editorial identity, provided its presentation does not turn invented experience into purported real testimony. Personality earns its place when it helps the viewer understand why this account would say this particular thing.

Duo is useful here as a study of continuity. The cited campaign gives an existing mascot another episode; it does not ask an unfamiliar face to become memorable in one post. The brand already has a character the audience can recognise. For a smaller account, the equivalent work is defining what the persona notices, wants and repeatedly misunderstands. That can be a fictional character or a real editorial voice, but the audience should not have to relearn the account every time it appears. Write three premises that only this account would plausibly use. If they all depend on copying Duo's behaviour, the persona is still borrowed.

Keep the performance claim separate from the creative lesson. A mascot story can make an app part of a cultural conversation without proving which viewers later subscribed. For your own account, decide what evidence would indicate that people understood the premise and what evidence would indicate useful product interest. Those are different stages. The practical test is to produce several original episodes with a stable character and different situations, then inspect whether the responses refer to the identity you intended to create. Do not equate recognisability with universal approval; a distinctive voice will also give some people a reason to leave.
