1 / 3 · YOUR OWN PACE

Include two examples of desired posts and one counterexample. State who approves, when to escalate uncertainty and where to record results. Keep the page short enough to use during work. A strategy document nobody opens does not prevent persona drift.

A useful check asks whether a new colleague could choose between two pieces using only the document. If both seem equally acceptable while only one fits the persona, a decision criterion is probably missing. Add the detail that settles the choice, then remove background paragraphs that never help decide. A brief becomes stronger through sharper boundaries, not automatically through extra length or more sophisticated vocabulary.

Make the account understandable to its next operator.

A useful account brief lets a new teammate make a reasonable editorial decision without asking the founder what the account is supposed to be. Name the intended viewer, recurring situation, promise and exclusions. Include a few examples of appropriate ideas and one tempting idea that does not belong. The counterexample often reveals the boundary more clearly than a list of positive adjectives.

Keep operating ownership beside the editorial brief: who can approve a post, who resolves an incident and where the current account record lives. Avoid turning the brief into a vault of credentials; reference the approved access process instead. Review the document when the account’s audience or purpose changes, and retain the reason for the change. A short current brief is more valuable than an elaborate one that describes a previous version of the project. Its job is to reduce ambiguous decisions at the moment work happens, not to decorate the onboarding folder.

The Uffizi collaboration makes a useful briefing exercise because two editorial worlds meet: a cultural institution and a musician. Before planning a similar original collaboration, write what each participant contributes that the other cannot supply alone. Then define the viewer's reason to watch, the permitted setting, the rights, the required credit and the destination, if any. A brief that only says film a creator in our location leaves the most important decision unresolved. After production, check whether the asset actually shows the intended relationship or merely documents that two names appeared in the same room.
