## The takeaway

Shadowban is an informal term for suspected distribution suppression without explicit notice. Here it remains a hypothesis unless evidence establishes it.

## The argument

Describe what changed: fewer views, a missing traffic source, a displayed restriction. Keep screenshots and dates. A missing explanation does not justify inventing platform intent. A precise timeline helps support or analysts work; a dramatic label provides little information.

Also preserve decisions made after the decline. Content changes, pauses or deletions can affect later observations. The objective is not to prove the first intuition but to keep the record interpretable. Precise language leaves room for competing explanations and new platform information. It also prevents an unverified suspicion from becoming a permanent account label that influences every future brief and analysis.

## A label should not arrive before the evidence.

When distribution changes abruptly, the pressure to name the cause is strong. Separate what is observed from what is inferred. A platform notification, a visible restriction and a drop in a metric are different pieces of information. Record them independently, with dates and the affected scope. The phrase shadowban can otherwise compress several unknowns into an explanation that nobody has actually tested.

Inspect publication status, account notices, content changes and the observation window before proposing a mechanism. Compare relevant history rather than the single best post the account ever produced. Avoid repeatedly changing multiple variables while investigating; doing so makes the next observation harder to interpret. If the available evidence cannot identify the cause, say exactly what remains unknown and what would help distinguish the plausible explanations. A careful diagnosis may be less satisfying than a confident theory, but it gives the operator a better next action.

### Keep observation and interpretation apart

- **Observed:** A dated status, notification or measurement.

- **Inferred:** A possible explanation that fits some of those facts.

- **Needed:** The evidence that would distinguish it from alternatives.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
