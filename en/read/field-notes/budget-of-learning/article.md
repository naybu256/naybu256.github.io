## The takeaway

A learning budget includes production, coordination, execution and analysis. It helps select interpretable experiments beyond publishing cost alone.

## The argument

Two equally priced tests can produce very different knowledge. One mixes accounts, formats and markets; another isolates a useful decision. Record full cost and expected decision before launching. A cheap but unreadable experiment can cost more than its quote suggests.

To choose between experiments, ask which decision each can actually change. If a test can neither alter allocation nor improve a brief, its value is uncertain. Exploratory campaigns may have other goals, but should name them. A budget becomes clearer when every cost connects to a question. This does not require predicting returns; it requires explaining what information would make the expenditure useful to the team.

## Count the cost of understanding the result.

A production budget that stops at rendering or publishing misses a large part of the experiment. Somebody has to choose the question, review the assets, resolve exceptions and interpret the observations. If those tasks are not funded, a cheap stream of output can become an expensive backlog of unexplained results. Estimate the time needed to close the learning loop before expanding the volume.

Compare proposals in terms of the decision they can help make. A small, carefully reviewed batch may answer whether an explanation is understandable; a larger batch may be needed for a different measurement question. Neither size is inherently more disciplined. Set a cost ceiling and a review date, record what would justify continuing, and preserve the option to stop without treating the stop as failure. The budget should buy a better decision under uncertainty. Publishing more material is useful only when the team can still explain what it is learning from it.

### The full cost of a test

- **Prepare:** Research, brief and production choices.

- **Operate:** Review, publication and exception handling.

- **Interpret:** Measurement, analysis and the next decision.
