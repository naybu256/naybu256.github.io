## The takeaway

Niche Warming means exploring niche content in the app through searches, viewing and authentic interactions. Its precise effect on reach remains a testable hypothesis.

## The argument

Preparation can help understand an audience’s codes as well as contextualize an account. Record topics, creators and recommendations encountered. A checklist of mechanical gestures does not prove relevant context formed. Compare comparable accounts afterward and retain observations contradicting your intuition.

A preparation notebook can record creator vocabulary, recurring comment questions and formats that have become ordinary. Those observations feed a concrete brief. Keep them separate from account outcomes: learning a niche’s language can provide research value even when no reach improvement is demonstrated. This distinction allows the team to use what it actually learned without turning the whole activity into an unsupported algorithmic claim.

## Learn the culture before prescribing a ritual.

Before an account publishes into a niche, somebody should understand the questions, references and recurring frustrations that make that niche legible. That is editorial research. Read a small, relevant set of public posts and note what people are trying to solve, which explanations are already common and where a useful perspective is missing. The resulting brief should contain observations, not a checklist of gestures performed to appease an algorithm.

Keep any claim about technical warming separate from that cultural work. If you want to test an operating routine, define it precisely, compare reasonably similar cohorts and record the creative material that follows it. Do not mix a better-researched account with an unrelated control and attribute the difference to warm-up actions. Research can improve the content without proving a hidden platform mechanism. The practical deliverable is a clearer audience promise and a small set of original ideas, followed by an experiment whose unknowns remain visible.

### Separate three different activities

- **Research:** Understand the public culture and problems of a niche.

- **Preparation:** Complete the legitimate account and operating setup.

- **Experiment:** Test a defined routine without assuming its effect.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.

## TokPortal workflow: Turn an audience niche into explicit warming topics

An account about freelance money problems needs a more precise brief than “business content”. These are example topic fields for the documented Advanced Niche Warming option, to be combined with the rest of a validated bundle request.

Endpoint: POST /api/ext/bundles — body fragment

```json
{
  "wants_advanced_warming": true,
  "advanced_warming_terms": [
    "freelancer budget",
    "subscription tracking",
    "irregular income"
  ]
}
```

The topics describe a proposed editorial neighbourhood. They do not establish a causal reach advantage. A suitable brief keeps the operator’s actions relevant to the account instead of treating warming as a magic ceremony.

- **Use valid targets:** Supply 3–30 distinct terms, in multiples of three, with 2–50 characters per term.

- **Include the flag:** Targets without wants_advanced_warming: true can return ADVANCED_WARMING_FLAG_REQUIRED.

- **Inspect the result:** Use the recorded proof/data report for operational review. Do not present completion as proof of future reach.
