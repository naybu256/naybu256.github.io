## The takeaway

A distribution failure can involve account access, a feature, removed content, a missing publication or weak reach. These states call for different responses.

## The argument

Start with observable facts. Does the account exist? Is the post accessible? Does a notification describe a restriction? Only then inspect performance. This separation avoids requesting new creative work when the original file was never published correctly.

A simple matrix can connect each state to a check: access for accounts, URL for posts, notification for restrictions and measurement window for reach. The team can then route the incident to the right owner. This reduces back-and-forth and protects creators from being asked to remake content to solve a technical problem. The classification earns its value through a concrete next action, not through more elaborate terminology.

## Find the stage that failed before optimising the next one.

A content workflow can fail before publication, during execution, in audience reach, or after a viewer clicks. These failures can produce the same complaint in a meeting: “it did not work”. Draw the chain and attach an observable state to each stage. A missing asset is not weak creative distribution. An irrelevant landing page is not necessarily a problem with the first frame.

Choose the next action based on the earliest unresolved stage. Confirm that the intended asset was published, then inspect the available distribution information, then consider whether attention became qualified interest. Keep uncertainty visible where analytics do not connect the stages. A useful retrospective can contain more than one failure, but it should not erase their order. Fixing a downstream metric while the upstream state is unknown tends to create more activity than learning. The point of the chain is to make one next decision precise enough to test.

### Where did the chain break?

- **Publication:** The intended object exists in the intended place.

- **Attention:** The available evidence describes who encountered it and when.

- **Continuation:** The next step fulfils the same promise and can be inspected.

Editorial model: a way to reason about the process, not measured platform results.
